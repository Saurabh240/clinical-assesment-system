Minor Ailment Assessment & Treatment Options - Nausea and Vomiting of Pregnancy
RATIONALE FOR PRESCRIBING
Patient presents with the following symptoms consistent with nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (Check all that apply)
Confirmed pregnancy
Onset in the first 9 weeks of gestation
Mild nausea with or without occasional vomiting
Personal history of GERD
Other:                                                                                                                             
Patient meets the following criteria for minor ailment prescribing (Must check all)
No red flags present (dehydration for >12 hours, unable to keep food down for >12 hours, signs of hyperemesis gravidarum - weight loss, relentless vomiting, excessive thirst, infrequent urination, dizziness, dark-coloured urine, extreme fatigue, confusion, yellow or bloody vomit)
Patient does not have diabetes
No fever, headache, vision changes, diarrhea, changes in bowel habits or vaginal bleeding
No abdominal pain or tenderness (besides discomfort attributed to retching)
Presents with mild to moderate NVP based on PUQE score - see page 2 for assessment
Not diagnosed iron deficiency anemia and unable to take iron supplement
Able to keep down oral medications to manage other medical conditions and able to maintain nutritional requirements
NVP Pharmacological Treatment
Doxylamine succinate/pyridoxine hydrochloride 10mg/10mg start with 2 tablets PO QHS, add 1 tablet QAM plus 1 tablet mid-afternoon as required
Pyridoxine 25 mg PO Q8H
Ginger tablet 250 mg PO Q6H
Dimenhydrinate 50 mg PO or PR Q4–6H PRN
Diphenhydramine 25 mg PO Q4–6H PRN
Promethazine 12.5 mg–25 mg PO Q4–6H PRN
Other Recommendations to Consider
Maintenance of fluid and electrolyte status
Eat small, bland, frequent meals of pregnancy safe foods the patient finds appealing
Eat at times of day when nausea is less severe
Avoid strong food odours
