Minor Ailment Assessment & Treatment Options - Threadworms Or Pinworms
RATIONALE FOR PRESCRIBING
Patient presents with the following symptoms consistent with threadworms or pinworms (Check all that apply)
Perianal or perineal pruritus and scratching
First episode or > 2 weeks since completed pinworm treatment and confirmed reinfection
Sleeplessness due to pruritus, irritability, teeth grinding, etc…
Confirmed diagnosis via testing (flashlight test, scotch-tape test, or microscopic analysis of subungual sample)
Caregiver of confirmed infected person, household member of confirmed infected person, symptomatic and confirmed close contact (e.g., letter sent home from daycare or school), or recent refugee or immigrant to Canada
Asymptomatic
Other:                                                                                                                             
Patient meets the following criteria for minor ailment prescribing (Must check all)
< 2 weeks since completed pinworm treatment and symptomatic
No systemic symptoms present (fever, poor appetite/weight loss, night sweats, heat/cold intolerance, fatigue, etc.)
No gastrointestinal symptoms (abdominal pain, severe diarrhea, blood in stool, dysuria (painful urination), decreased urine output, change in colour of stool or urine, etc.)
No rash or broken skin/lesions (potentially from excessive scratching or secondary bacterial infection)
Patient has normal bowel habits
No burning, swelling, or rectal bleeding
Patient is not pregnant
Patient is > 2 years old
Prescription Treatment
Mebendazole: Adults and Children ≥ 2 years old: 100mg as a single dose, repeat after two and four weeks (Days 0, 14, 28; Total 3 doses)
OTC Treatment
Pyrantel Pamoate: Adults and Children ≥ 2 years old: 11 mg/kg base as a single dose, repeat in two weeks maximum: 1 g (Days 0 and 14; Total 2 doses)
***Note: Since pinworms can be passed from one person to another, some experts suggest treating all close contacts of a person with pinworms even if they are asymptomatic to prevent reinfection and spread of pinworms. This is especially important in households where more than one member is infected or where repeated, symptomatic infections occur.
